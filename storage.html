Energy storage

The buffers that make a hybrid plant work.

Three stores sit between an intermittent resource and a synthesis loop that must not stop: a battery for hours, a hydrogen store for days, a heat battery for the reformer's steam. The optimizer sizes all three per site — here is what it builds, how they cycle, and what a single Li-ion cell behind the battery number looks like.

01 — Across the fleet

What the optimizer builds, plant by plant.

Every modelled plant, at one carbon-intensity target and pathway: how many build each store and how large. Storage is endogenous — it appears where the local resource and prices make it worth more than reformer backup or grid import.

02 — One plant, hour by hour

How the stores actually cycle.

From the plant's hourly dispatch: equivalent full cycles per year, hours in use, state of charge — and one week of the year to see the rhythm. These are the optimizer's own decisions, not a rule of thumb.

03 — Cell lab

Inside the battery: design one cell, scale it to the plant.

A bottom-up Li-ion cell: choose chemistry, format and electrode design; the model builds the electrode stack, winds or stacks it into the cell, and returns capacity, energy density, mass and a materials bill — then multiplies it out to the battery the optimizer sized for the plant above. Simplified after the BatPaC / OpenCell approach; every constant is on screen.

What this is and is not

Areal capacity = loading × specific capacity; electrode thickness from loading, composite density and porosity; the stack fills the format's inner volume; electrolyte fills the pores. It reproduces commercial cells to within roughly 10 % (a 21700 NMC 811 at 18–20 mg/cm² lands near 4.8–5 Ah and 260–280 Wh/kg). Not modelled: rate capability, cycle life, thermal limits, formation losses, manufacturing cost — the optimizer's installed $/kWh covers those empirically.
