Method

Framing

HOPS is a single mixed-integer linear program per plant-scenario. Capacity choices and 8,760 hours of operation are decided simultaneously, because in a hybrid plant they are not separable: the value of an electrolyser depends on how the synthesis loop is allowed to ramp, and the value of storage depends on the hourly shape of the local wind and solar resource.

The carbon-intensity target enters as a constraint rather than a price. Sweeping the target from business-as-usual down to near-zero traces the cost-versus-carbon frontier for that plant; the Economic Decarbonization Threshold is where the frontier crosses the unsubsidised conventional cost.

Objective and constraints

The objective minimises levelized cost of ammonia over the modelled year: annualised capital charges on endogenous capacities plus hourly variable cost, net of any grid export revenue.

(1)min   LCOA  =  [ Σu CRFu · capexu · Cu  +  Σt ctᵀxt ] / MNH₃
Cu
installed capacity of unit u (endogenous, continuous)
xt
vector of hourly flows and states in hour t
ct
hourly cost vector: electricity, gas, CO₂ transport and storage, variable O&M
CRFu
capital recovery factor from WACC and unit lifetime
MNH₃
annual ammonia production, fixed to the plant's nameplate demand

The carbon constraint caps annual emissions per unit of product, counting process CO₂, captured fractions, imported grid electricity at its hourly emissions factor, and upstream methane leakage.

(2)Σt etᵀxt  ≤  CItarget · MNH₃

Remaining constraints are conventional: mass and energy balances at each node, capacity limits, storage state-of-charge dynamics with round-trip losses, ramp limits and minimum stable load on the reformer and synthesis loop (the integer part), and grid connection limits in both directions.

System boundary

Plant fleet

The fleet is ~91 existing ammonia plants in the United States and Europe, each with its own location, nameplate capacity, market zone and local renewable resource. Retrofit logic starts from the existing reformer rather than a greenfield site, so the threshold reflects the capital already in the ground.

Solving

Each plant-scenario is one MILP with 8,760 hourly time steps solved at full resolution — no representative days or clustering, since the threshold is sensitive to the tails of the resource distribution. Sweeping the carbon target and the fleet gives [VALUE] solves per published run; solver, version and settings are recorded with each run.

Limitations

The results are useful for comparing plants and pathways, not for underwriting a project. In particular:

What this model does not do
  • Perfect foresight. The optimizer sees the whole year at once, so it overstates achievable arbitrage and understates the cost of forecast error.
  • Price taking. Electricity and gas prices are exogenous; large-scale deployment would move them, and that feedback is absent.
  • Deterministic, single weather year. One resource and price year per run. Interannual variability is not propagated into the reported threshold unless a multi-year run is stated.
  • Linearised process engineering. Efficiencies are constant or piecewise; part-load behaviour of the synthesis loop is approximated by minimum load and ramp limits.
  • No grid or permitting constraints beyond a connection limit. Interconnection queues, land availability and local siting limits are out of scope.
  • Costs are levelized, not financed. A single WACC stands in for real capital structure, contracting and offtake risk.
  • Emissions accounting is attributional. Grid imports are charged at an hourly average factor, not a marginal or consequential one; results shift if that choice changes.

Where a limitation materially moves the threshold, the results page reports the sensitivity rather than a single number.
